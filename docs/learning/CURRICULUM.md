# Learning curriculum (ADR-002)

The product is the owner's understanding of DSP and radio protocols, shown in devlog write-ups.
Bandlantern is the lab bench: it captures, replays, and visualizes, and each module's code plugs
into it. Protocols are picked for the **new concept** each one teaches, not to grow a mode count.

## The module loop
Every module runs the same steps. A module is done when all of them are.

1. **Scope** (one short session with Claude): what the signal is, which concepts are new, the
   references, what to capture, and the exit questions written down up front.
2. **Study:** read the spec or paper sections and derive the maths on paper. AI-written versions
   of the A0 and Track A blocks exist in the private predecessor repo (ADR-001); don't open them
   until your own block works. Afterwards they're a second opinion to compare against.
3. **Modulator first** (Python, digital modes): synthesize the signal from the spec. That gives
   ground truth, and it's half the understanding.
4. **Demodulator or decoder** (Python): get it working on synthetic IQ, then add impairments one
   at a time (AWGN at a set SNR, frequency offset, timing drift, multipath, clipping).
5. **Real captures:** record SigMF with the bench, log what was heard, and decode it.
6. **Measure:** decode rate or BER vs SNR, false decodes per hour, and the gap to theory or a
   published figure. These plots are the centre of the post.
7. **Golden vectors** exported from your Python.
8. **C++ port** into the engine (a DSP block, or an `IDecoder` with probe/configure/process/reset):
   real-time safe, matching your vectors, with a fuzz test for decoders and a benchmark.
9. **Exit interview:** Claude asks about 8 interview-style questions ("why this loop bandwidth?",
   "what breaks at 1% sample-rate error?"). Gaps go back to step 2.
10. **Write-up:** published on the devlog (template below).

## Claude's role in modules (tutor mode)
- Explains prerequisites, points to spec sections and papers, asks questions before giving
  answers, and gives hints in increasing steps: a nudge, then the concept, then pseudo-maths.
- **Doesn't write the module's DSP or decoder code** (Python or C++) unless asked for a specific
  piece. If it does, the post's AI-use note says so.
- Reviews your code (correctness, real-time safety, numerical issues) and runs the tests.
- Builds the plumbing: engine wiring, UI panels for decoder output, CI, file I/O, plot helpers.
- On request, plants a realistic bug in a copy of a block for you to find.
- Edits posts for clarity only when asked. The voice is yours.

## Shared lab tools (write once in Track A, reuse)
- An **impairment channel** (owner): AWGN at an exact SNR (state the noise bandwidth), frequency
  offset, sample-rate offset, multipath taps, phase noise.
- A **measurement harness** (owner): sweep SNR, run trials, and plot the decode rate with
  confidence intervals.
- **Plot and figure scripts** (Claude helps): every figure in a post is regenerated by a script in
  `tools/py/learning/<module>/`.

## Tracks and modules
Each module is listed with its new concepts, a starting reference, and what to capture. The
order matters within a track. A0 and Track A come first because the bench needs them: the
waterfall appears with A0 and the first audio with A1 plus a demodulator.

### Track A — Build the receiver (analog)
- [ ] **A0 Spectrum and the pipeline (warm-up).** Window choice (scalloping loss, equivalent noise
  bandwidth, sidelobe level), FFT scaling to dBFS, resolution bandwidth vs bin spacing, power
  vs log averaging, overlap. Plus the playback pipeline: a reader thread and a DSP thread joined
  by the SPSC ring, real-time safe. The FFT itself comes from pocketfft. Ref: harris, "On the
  Use of Windows for Harmonic Analysis with the DFT" (1978); Heinzel, Rüdiger, Schilling,
  "Spectrum and spectral density estimation by the DFT" (2002). Capture: synthetic tones, then
  any FM broadcast span. Lights up the waterfall.
- [ ] **A1 DDC and decimation.** NCO and mixing, CIC (Hogenauer 1981) vs polyphase FIR decimation,
  aliasing and image budgets, filter design trade-offs, rational resampling. Plus the receive
  chain (the order of NB, DDC, resampler, squelch, demod, AGC), the audio jitter buffer, and the
  clock-drift loop that keeps it level; they come due with the first demodulator. Ref: Lyons, *Understanding DSP*;
  harris, *Multirate Signal Processing*. Capture: any FM broadcast span.
- [ ] **A2 AM and the PLL.** Envelope detection, then **synchronous AM** with a PLL (loop filter
  design, lock range, the phase detector). Ref: Gardner, *Phaselock Techniques*; Rice, *Digital
  Communications: A Discrete-Time Approach*. Capture: airband, MW/SW broadcast.
- [ ] **A3 FM, stereo, and RDS.** Discriminators (atan2 differentiation vs polar), de-emphasis
  (75 µs in the US), pilot PLL, L−R DSB-SC at 38 kHz; then **RDS**: BPSK at 1187.5 bit/s on 57 kHz,
  differential coding, 26-bit blocks with offset words and syndrome checks. This module bridges
  analog and digital. Ref: IEC 62106 / NRSC-4 (RBDS). Capture: strong local FM stations.
- [ ] **A4 SSB, CW, AGC, and the noise blanker.** Phasing vs filter vs Weaver (1956) methods, Hilbert
  transformers, AGC attack and decay dynamics, impulse blanking. Ref: Weaver 1956; Lyons.
  Capture: HF SSB and CW through the Ham It Up.
- [ ] **A5 Spectrogram zoom (optional).** A DDC per view to the zoom centre, then a finer FFT;
  what zoom costs vs a longer FFT. Builds on A0 and A1.

### Track B — Digital fundamentals
- [ ] **B1 APRS (Bell 202 AFSK + AX.25).** FSK demodulation (mark 1200 Hz / space 2200 Hz), clock
  recovery, NRZI, bit stuffing, HDLC framing, CRC-16-CCITT. Ref: the AX.25 2.2 spec and the APRS
  1.0.1 spec. Capture: 144.390 MHz.
- [ ] **B2 RTTY and CW decoding.** Baudot/ITA2 at 45.45 baud with 170 Hz shift; Morse timing
  classification with adaptive dot length (a statistics problem more than a DSP one). Capture: HF
  contests and beacons.
- [ ] **B3 PSK31.** BPSK at 31.25 baud with raised-cosine shaping, differential encoding, a
  **Costas loop**, Gardner timing recovery (1986), varicode. Ref: Rice ch. 7–8; G3PLX's PSK31
  description. Capture: HF PSK sub-bands.
- [ ] **B4 ADS-B.** Pulse-position modulation at 1 Mbit/s, preamble detection, CRC-24 with
  single-bit error correction, CPR position decoding. Ref: Junzi Sun, *The 1090 MHz Riddle*
  (free book; don't open its companion code). Capture: 1090 MHz.
- [ ] **B5 AIS (optional, needs ships in range).** GMSK (BT 0.4) at 9600 bit/s, matched filtering,
  HDLC again. Ref: ITU-R M.1371. Capture: 161.975 / 162.025 MHz.

### Track C — Weak signals and coding
- [ ] **C1 WSPR.** 4-FSK at about 1.46 baud, a K=32 rate-1/2 convolutional code with **sequential
  (Fano) decoding**, interleaving, a sync vector, time alignment. Ref: G4JNT, "The WSPR Coding
  Process". Capture: 30 m / 20 m WSPR frequencies.
- [ ] **C2 FT8.** 8-GFSK at 6.25 baud, Costas-array sync search across time and frequency,
  **LDPC (174,91)** with belief propagation, CRC-14, 77-bit message packing; a decode-rate vs SNR
  curve compared with published WSJT-X figures. Ref: Franke, Somerville, Taylor, "The FT4 and
  FT8 Communication Protocols", QEX 2020. The flagship post.
- [ ] **C3 Viterbi in miniature.** A K=3 or K=7 convolutional code with hard- and soft-decision
  Viterbi, BER vs Eb/N0 against theory. Prepares for D2.

### Track D — Wideband and modern waveforms
- [ ] **D1 LoRa.** Chirp spread spectrum: dechirp plus FFT, Gray mapping, Hamming codes,
  whitening, diagonal interleaving. Ref: Knight & Seeber 2016; Tapparel et al. 2020 (the papers,
  not their GPL code). Capture: 902–928 MHz (Meshtastic beacons, PHY and headers only).
- [ ] **D2 Weather satellite LRPT.** QPSK/OQPSK, carrier and timing recovery at 72 kbit/s, CCSDS
  framing: Viterbi (K=7), derandomization, Reed–Solomon (255,223). Ref: CCSDS blue books
  (free). Capture: a 137 MHz pass with a V-dipole or QFH (check which satellites are live).
- [ ] **D3 OFDM from scratch.** Build your own modem first: cyclic prefix, Schmidl–Cox
  synchronization, pilot-based channel estimation, equalization. Then a real OFDM signal if one
  is receivable (DRM shortwave).

### Track E — Recognition
- [ ] **E1 Feature-based modulation recognition.** Higher-order cumulants (Swami & Sadler 2000),
  cyclostationary features, a spectral-correlation view; a classical classifier.
- [ ] **E2 A learned classifier.** A small CNN trained on your synthetic modulators from Tracks A–D
  (they're the dataset) plus labeled captures; export to ONNX and run it in C++. Compare it
  with E1 honestly.
- [ ] **E3 Direction finding (optional, needs coherent hardware).** MUSIC/Bartlett DoA, array
  calibration.

## Post template
1. **What the signal is:** where you hear it and what it carries, with a waterfall screenshot.
2. **The maths:** derivations in your own words, with the spec or paper sections cited.
3. **Prototype:** key code excerpts and the synthetic-signal plots.
4. **On the air:** real captures, what decoded, and what didn't.
5. **What went wrong:** bugs, misconceptions, and dead ends. Usually the best part.
6. **Measurements:** decode rate vs SNR, comparison to theory or published figures, CPU in C++.
7. **References.**
8. **AI-use note:** what Claude did (tutoring, review, plumbing, any marked code).

## Rules (from ADR-002)
- Clean-room (ADR-004): specs, papers, datasheets, and books only. No reading other implementations
  (GPL or otherwise) before your own works. Afterwards, compare outputs and published
  numbers, not code.
- Only lawful captures and publication: amateur, broadcast, ADS-B, AIS, weather satellites, ISM
  sensors. No pagers, cellular, cordless, encrypted, or trunked public-safety voice.
- C++ in the live path stays real-time safe; golden vectors and fuzz tests as before.
